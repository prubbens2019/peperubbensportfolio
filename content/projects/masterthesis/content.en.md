I chose the master's in Strategic Product Design because I was looking for the combination of design and strategy. My bachelor's in Industrial Design Engineering gave me the technical and design foundation, but I noticed I was just as interested in why something gets built and how it fits within a larger organisation or market.

I spent half a year of my master's in Copenhagen, doing research in preparation for my thesis. The most valuable insight there was seeing how another country approaches digital government services. Denmark is ahead on a number of fronts, and the Danish business portal Virk.dk became a concrete reference point for me rather than just a theoretical ideal.

For my thesis I worked from within the Ministry of the Interior and Kingdom Relations on the question of how contact between government and entrepreneurs could run more smoothly. The hardest part was not the research itself, but keeping it concrete. A topic like the relationship between government and entrepreneurs can easily become endlessly abstract if you are not careful. I spent a lot of time repeatedly bringing the research back to something tangible, something you can actually show someone instead of just an analysis on paper.
