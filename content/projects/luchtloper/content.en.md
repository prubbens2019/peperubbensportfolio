For the design competition around a replacement for Rotterdam's Luchtsingel, I developed the Luchtloper concept: a pedestrian bridge with a steel support structure and a replaceable, 3D-printed polymer shell. Unlike many other projects, I worked on this entirely on my own, from first concept to an outline for a design handbook.

The choice for that combination came from two things. A shell you can replace or adjust separately, without having to rebuild the entire structure, suits a bridge that needs to last decades in a changing city. And a 3D-printed shell can be made with less material waste than a traditionally cast or milled part, and is easier to replace at the end of its life than rebuilding the whole bridge.

The main requirement from the competition itself was feasibility: it had to be a realistically buildable concept, not science fiction. That meant constantly shifting between ambition and realism. Coming up with an exciting idea is not hard, but coming up with an idea a jury of engineers and civil servants could take seriously as genuinely buildable requires just as much technical grounding as creativity. The entry was not a ready-made construction design, but a concept for a design handbook.
