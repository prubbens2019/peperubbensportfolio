At kwiek. mobility I led a concept to make mobility scooters easier to share among elderly residents of care homes. I came up with the concept myself and prepared the presentation for the tender. I also kept an overview of how my teammates handled the engineering and finances, which sounds less exciting than building something with your own hands but mattered just as much. Someone had to make sure everything came together into one proposal.

The hardest part of the project was balancing cost against feasibility. A shared-scootmobile concept sounds simple, but as soon as you develop it for a municipality you run into questions like who maintains the scooters and who is liable in case of an accident. On top of that comes the question of how much it can all cost before it stops being financially viable. Constantly shifting between an idea that sounds good and an idea that is actually workable within a realistic budget, that was ultimately what it came down to.

We submitted the concept for a tender issued by the Municipality of The Hague and placed second. Not a win, but a result I still look back on with real satisfaction. It proved the concept held up under the scrutiny of an actual government procurement process. That is exactly what I want this project remembered for: that I can take an idea from a first sketch to a concrete, defensible proposal, with the user always at the centre, and that I can work with a government body without losing edge or ambition along the way.
