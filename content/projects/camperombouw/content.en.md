The camper conversion did not start out of necessity, but out of wanting to give myself a big hands-on challenge. Buying a Renault Master with no windows, no floor and no electrics, and converting it entirely myself into something I could actually live in, seemed like a good way to put everything I had learned in my studies and other projects into practice, without a client or a deadline set by someone else.

The biggest problem along the way was moisture and insulation. Insulating a metal van without creating condensation that eventually rots wood is harder than it sounds. My design background came in handy here: I could think systematically about material layers and air circulation instead of just guessing. Even so, I spent a lot of time researching the right materials, and even more time checking whether it actually worked as intended, since with insulation you often only find out after a few months.

What I am most proud of is that everything just works. There are solar panels on the roof, which means I can be away for weeks at a time without ever needing a power outlet, even though the lights, a proper fridge and a ventilation system running continuously all draw a fair amount of power. The proof that it works, every time I open the door and step inside, is exactly why this project still gives me the most satisfaction.
