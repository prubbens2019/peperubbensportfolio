Waardevolle Gezelligheid started out of pure entrepreneurial itch, together with a friend. No elaborate business plan upfront, more the idea to build a bar and rent it out for parties. What followed was a project where design and running a small business constantly overlapped.

The bar depended on an on-site power outlet. The tap system needed to cool and pump continuously, and the biggest technical challenge was connecting reliably to whatever power supply a location had, from a regular outdoor socket to something improvised at a garden party.

The most memorable moment did not actually come from the bar itself, but from how we found new clients. We did active outreach at the drinks and parties where we were already working, simply talking to people and asking if they wanted to book us too. That turned out so effective that we never seriously had to invest in online marketing. For two years, most of our new business came from word of mouth on the floor of the previous party.

The project mainly taught me how closely design and operations are tied together. A bar that looks good but does not work on location is worthless, and a sales approach that never gets past an idea on paper delivers nothing.
