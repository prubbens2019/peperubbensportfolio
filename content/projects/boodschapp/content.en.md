Two people, one supermarket and a list that was never right. That was the design problem Boodschapp started with. I built a shared grocery list for my girlfriend and me, with a pantry that thinks along and recipes you add to the list with a single link. I still enjoy how little it took to solve such an everyday annoyance.

It started with an observation: shopping together often goes wrong in small moments. Double purchases, forgotten items and a list in a chat that nobody keeps up to date. Boodschapp tackles that with one shared list that syncs live.

The list (image 1) is sorted by the walking route of a Dutch supermarket. Products are placed in the right aisle with AI, and you can drag them if the layout turns out differently. Every user has their own colour, so you immediately see who added or ticked off an item, and a button at the top shows who is in the store right now.

The pantry (image 2) is the part I learned the most from. For each product you switch with one tap between in stock, almost out and out. Anything almost out or out appears on the list automatically, so you no longer have to think about it.

With recipes (image 3) you paste a link. The app pulls out the ingredients and puts them next to your pantry. In one overview you see what you already have, what is almost out and what you still need to buy. Recipes you want to keep go into your recipe book. Technically it is a React app with Firebase, installable as a PWA on your phone.
